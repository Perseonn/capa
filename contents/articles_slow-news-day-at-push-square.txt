@title: Slow News Day at Push Square
@author: Perseonn Balthasaar
@updated: 2026-10-17

I was going through my morning routine of looking for video game news. As usual, I went through This Week in Video Games, VGC, then Push Square. Now when I reached Push Square, there's this featured article, titled "Going Platinum: Stray Is Probably the Only Game I've Speedran for a Trophy". 

Huh. Must be a slow news day at Push Square if Stray, a game released in July 2022, is getting featured in October 2026.

But it gets worse. Article should be available at [[link https://www.pushsquare.com/features/going-platinum-stray-is-probably-the-only-game-ive-speedran-for-a-trophy | Push Square🔗]]

[[image featured-at-push-square.avif | Top-right. ]]

+ Reading the Article
The headline makes it sound as if the trophy hunt itself is the story, rather than the game. That's already a fairly thin premise for a featured article.

Right at the start, the author reveals that the trophy was earned on 23 July 2022, yet the feature is being published in October 2026. Essentially, we're getting a four-year-old platinum journal entry, complete with a detour into the author's cats.

Oh, did I say journal entry? Perhaps I was being too kind. There are only two sentences about the trophy journey itself:

> One Trophy concerns the first time you're chased by those horrid little creatures called Zurks; you need to escape them without once being caught. I remember that being a pain point for many people.

Moreover, the author uses promotional screenshots instead of screenshots from his own playthrough. There's something inherently wrong with that in a personal retrospective. It's as if the author is afraid of saying, "I actually played this, and here's something worth showing you."

Instead, we get a two-sentence personal anecdote accompanied by the publisher's promotional photography.

Jeez, some effort would be nice. What is this? Some sort of nepotism or favoritism that got this article promoted to a featured spot? I wonder what happened in the editorial room...
